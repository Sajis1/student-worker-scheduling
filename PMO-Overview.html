What It Does

One system, two sides — students manage their own info, managers run the schedule.

Student Portal

  • Picks their name from a list — no separate login to manage
  • Submits and edits their own class schedule, including expected graduation term
  • Submits time-off requests and tracks their status
  • Gets emailed automatically the moment a request is approved or denied

Manager Dashboard

  • Live weekly calendar across S700, TLS, S701, and Back Office
  • One click auto-generates a full week's schedule, following every rule at right
  • Add, edit, duplicate, or delete any shift manually — overrides always survive the next auto-generate
  • Approve or deny time off in one click; the student is notified automatically
  • Per-student weekly-hours bar, tracked against that student's own cap
  • Copy for Excel reproduces the PMO's existing printable schedule — extensions, phone contacts, supervisor list, hours, and grad term included
  • Gets emailed automatically whenever a student submits a new time-off request

Scheduling Rules Applied Every Generate

In priority order — each rule only kicks in once everything above it has had first claim on the week.

  1. S700 & TLS come first, always. Full 8 AM–5 PM, Monday–Friday coverage there outranks even spreading hours evenly across the team.
  2. Never during class. A student's submitted class schedule blocks that time, plus a 15-minute buffer on both sides to get to/from work.
  3. 2-hour minimum shift, no exceptions — a gap that can't be filled with at least 2 hours is left as a reported gap rather than assigned to anyone.
  4. 20-hour weekly cap by default, adjustable per student (e.g. to 35) anytime a manager needs to, directly in Smartsheet.
  5. One shift per day. No split shifts. A full 8–5 day includes an unpaid 1-hour lunch, so only 8 hours count toward the cap.
  6. Home seat first. A student is only moved off their primary location once their own seat's people genuinely can't cover the gap.
  7. No more than a few different people per seat per day — S700 caps at 3, TLS and S701 at 2. Once a seat hits its cap that day, the rest is left as a reported gap rather than pulling in one more person.
  8. S701 is the flex seat — filled last, from whatever capacity remains, except it jumps to top priority on any day one person is covering S700 alone all day (so someone's nearby for their lunch).
  9. Escalation order for gaps: mix in other Front Desk staff, then Floaters, then Back Office — Floaters are the only backup S701 ever gets.
  10. Bonus 2nd S700 seat, filled last, purely to help whoever's furthest behind on hours catch up.
  11. 13-hour weekly floor, guaranteed where availability allows — a final pass tops up anyone still under 13 hours, home location first then moving around, spread across the week.
  12. Managers always have final say. Any shift can be added, changed, or removed by hand at any time.
